# TOUCHED CHIP (Paul, 2026-08-12)

## 1. RE-ROLL IS SELECTION-SCOPED (revision)
🎲 RE-ROLL re-rolls **only the selected rung(s)**; unselected rungs
PERSIST (your curated keeps survive). Nothing selected = the button
dims. Whole-ladder regeneration remains STAGE THE GRID's job — the
two buttons now differ by scope, not just source, and the
re-stage-destroys-curation gotcha shrinks to the one button that
says so.

## 2. DEPLOYMENT DOES NOT PROMOTE (revision of the provisional pin)
Applying to the play grid **no longer promotes provisionals to the
cast** (COPY ROWS would flood eight in). Deployed variations stay
PROVISIONAL — anonymous session players, fully functional on
stage, editable only via §3. **The cast is user-curated, always.**
(The old pin's promote-on-apply is superseded.)

## 3. THE LAST TOUCHED CHIP (Paul's design — the promotion gateway
## + the focus bridge)
- A small swatch in the machine column's header area: **LAST:
  [▢]** — passively tracks the colour of the last cell touched on
  ANY grid (staging rungs, play picks/mutes/voicings, workbench
  strokes). Tiny, silent, no selection churn.
- **PUSH IT** → if the colour is already in the cast: SELECT it
  (the machine column re-points — editing focus). If it's a
  provisional/unlisted: **ADD it to the cast** (next free slot;
  full cast = refuse + flash) AND select it. One push = "who IS
  that? — bring them into the band."
- **FOCUS RULES (the answer to "what brings the machine into
  focus")**: only deliberate acts — a CAST tap, a STAGING rung
  tap, or the LAST TOUCHED push. **Play-grid touches never
  auto-focus** (performance taps stay pure performance).
- Known quirk, accepted (DD precedent): identifying a lane cell
  costs a mute-toggle (its tap performs). The chip captures the
  identity either way; tap-tap restores. If it nags on device, a
  long-press-identify is the parked alternative.
- The footer ID chip remains the "which machine am I editing"
  truth (dashed ring for provisionals); LAST TOUCHED is the
  bridge that feeds it.
— design-side Claude

## 4. THE AUDITION MODE TOGGLE (Paul, 2026-08-12 — explicitly
## awaiting his hands; the toggle IS the experiment)
- Staging is PER PART ✓. While staging PLAYS, a small chip on the
  staging handle chooses what happens to THIS part's deployed
  material on the perform grid:
  - **ALONGSIDE (default)** — nothing mutes; the stage plays whole.
    Required by the B-LINE flow (writing the next lane against the
    part's deployed lanes is the two-grid design's core workflow —
    this default protects it).
  - **IN PLACE** — the part's deployed bands MUTE while staging
    plays (boundary-deferred, restored on stop): the candidate
    auditions IN THE PART'S SEAT — the true A/B for revision,
    no doubling mud.
- Other parts' material is NEVER touched by either mode (the bed
  is the bed). The toggle is audition config: not in undo, not in
  scenes.
- Paul's word after device time picks the shipping default (or
  kills one mode); until then both exist behind one small chip.

## 5. THE I/O ASYMMETRY (Paul, 2026-08-12 — ruled: emitters are NOT
## per part)
- **THE PART OWNS THE DOOR; THE MACHINE OWNS THE WIRE.**
  - RECEIVER = per part (a part IS one held question; the part
    header shows [IN: R1▾] + the piano; colours inherit the door).
  - EMITTER = per machine/colour, as this week's colour-owned
    ruling already has it. The build column's OUTPUT step is
    unchanged — it edits the selected colour.
- **Soft default, not ownership**: a NEW colour pre-fills its
  emitter from the part's last-used one (convenience; fully
  editable; never inherited-live).
- What this dissolves: the alt-family wrinkle vanishes whole
  (SPLIT TO-ALT · chop dests · PING keep full A–D addressing);
  no supersession of colour-owned routing; the part header drops
  its OUT chip.
- The band-level emitter OVERRIDE (the seat's mic) remains the
  future optional layer on the perform grid — unbuilt, unblocked.
- The metaphor, corrected: a part is WHO'S ASKING; the machines
  each decide WHERE THEIR ANSWER GOES. The band forms at the
  wires, as the thesis always had it.
