## THE INVENTORY (grouped; ratified-but-unbuilt included)

### Inputs (4 receivers)
Five modes per input: LATCH (notes toggle into a held pool) ·
HOLD (chord detect) · KEYS (on-screen keyboard) · REPLAY (loops
the last N passes of what you played) · FILE (a MIDI file plays
as the input). Per-input channel/block · octave · velocity. THE
COMPLEMENT SYSTEM: type a palette; EXCLUDE another input (plays
everything the chord isn't) or ONLY (consonance-locked). MPE
toggle. Multi-cable AUM routing.

### Processors (~35 cards · 7 groups)
- MELODY: ARP (patterns · octaves · oct-direction · anchored
  random · the euclid mask: rests/ties + march/wait walk) ·
  CASCADE · STRUM · GLIDE (bend | synth-portamento | stepped).
- HARMONY: HARMONIZE (3 voices) · TUTTI COIN · TUTTI PATTERN ·
  SPLIT · DRONE.
- RHYTHM: RATCHET ×3 (coin: size-weights lane · refire gap ·
  quota · velocity-coupled odds) · BURST ×3 (carry-spans) ·
  EUCLID (K-of-N · pick modes · invert · multi-line: up to 8
  targeted euclids in one card) · WEAVE ×4 (per-note clocks
  incl. per-note euclid) · PASSES (per-lap switchboard:
  play/mute/bypass/divert) · CHANCE (+ per-step odds lane).
- DYNAMICS: HUMANIZE · VELOCITY (scale + accent lane).
- CONTROL: LFO · FOLLOWER · STEP MOD · ENVELOPE · CC IN; MOD
  spans cells + targets the chain's own params (sequence your
  own gate).
- TIME: ECHO (through-chain echo routing) · SHIFT · LENGTH.
- UTILITY: OCTAVE · TRANSPOSE · CHANNEL · NUDGE.
- THE RIFF FAMILY: RIFF (the 303-style rank matrix — sequences
  chord POSITIONS, accent/slide/oct/tie lanes; the acid line
  follows the chord) · CAPTURE (play a line once → chord-
  relative forever) · MOTIF (plays phrases from the library,
  restrike|legato).

### Outputs & relationships (4 emitters)
CLAIM (own a part; nothing doubles it) · DUCK · TURNS (deals
hits across emitters). Per-output chains — THE RACK, 4
switchable SETUPs (mono · pocket · the treatment stack).
Mid-chain TAP (dry + processed layers from one machine, to any
output). Per-chain CHANNEL override (multitimbral from one port).

### Grids & workflow
The staged ladder (8 machines per part; banked by the carriage —
tweak, deal, walk back) · parts with per-part RATE and LENGTH
(polymeter/polyrhythm in one piece) · scenes · MUTATE (seeded
variation) · RANDOMIZE (ensemble-aware) · SPANS (patterns
cycling 1–8 columns or across passes) · the matrices + lanes
(state matrices · slider lanes · TIMING lane: the drawn pocket ·
DEST matrix: hocket by geometry · MUTE matrix) · undo everywhere
· no wrong wiring (loops fall silent).

### Performance — ROW 8
Eight assignable pads: STUTTER · FREEZE · HALFTIME (÷2/×2 the
grid) · REDIRECT · SWAP · BROADCAST · KILL · SETUP switch ·
MACRO fire · input latch acts · PART replay. Factory-loaded on a
danger gradient; fully re-configurable on its own page.

### Capture & the reel
Every pass recorded automatically (retro — never arm) · the pass
browser (per-pass piano rolls · first-change labels) · SAVE any
pass as MIDI (share sheet) · REVISIT any pass WITH ITS SETTINGS
(append-only time travel) · partial-pass honesty ·
boundary-clean playback.

### Discovery
THE PICK GRID: 64 complete machines auditioning against YOUR
held chord · deal straight to parts · layer combinations ·
RE-DEAL from a pregenerated corpus of thousands · the library ·
live preset preview · every panel teaching in place (plain-
English descriptions · truth strips showing each stage's in/out).

### Foundation & compatibility
AUv3 for AUM and friends · deterministic engine (same input,
same music — everything replayable) · host-sync + swing ·
microtonal-compatible by construction (your synth's tuning
decides the pitches) · MIDI file import AND export · light on
CPU · saves with the session.
— relayed by design-side Claude

## §2 — THE POSITIONING LINES (Paul, RATIFIED 2026-08-26)
The copy's two poles:
- **The gap**: "You already have an arpeggiator. This is what
  happens between them." (standing tagline)
- **The identity**: "Most MIDI tools are something you set up
  and leave. 8x8 State is one that you play."
Calibrations, on record:
- "Most" is the honest word (played MIDI tools exist: Fugue
  Machine, Atom 2, Cells, Drambo — all SEQUENCERS/launchers that
  OWN their notes). The armoured form when the category is
  named: **"MIDI processors are things you set up and leave —
  this one you play."** A played PROCESSOR — the performance
  surface operating on your live harmony — essentially doesn't
  exist elsewhere.
- The forum drawer (ready, verbatim): "Fugue Machine and Atom
  are played, sure — and they're sequencers that own their
  notes. This is a processor. It plays yours."

## §3 — USE CASES FOR MARKETING (Paul-commissioned, 2026-08-26;
## simple language; the raw sheet — cut down per surface)

### Play alone, sound like a band
1. Hold one chord — get a bassline, arps, and pads on four
   different synths at once.
2. Let one synth OWN the melody so nothing else plays the same
   notes.
3. Make the pads automatically get quieter whenever the bass
   plays.
4. Split one melody between two synths, trading note for note.
5. Play chords with your left hand while the app answers with
   fills in the gaps.
6. Hold a chord and get interlocking drum patterns where the kit
   is your chord.

### The acid and groove crowd
7. Draw a 303-style line once — it follows every chord you play
   after.
8. Real slides and accents, driven by your synth's own glide.
9. Drums that almost repeat but keep shifting — patterns of
   different lengths cycling against each other.
10. Rolls and fills that are random, but never twice in a row
    and never too many.
11. Hit harder, get more rolls — the dice listen to your hands.
12. Half-time the whole track with one pad. Bring it back on the
    drop.

### Play and capture
13. Play a lick once — the app knows it in every key, over
    chords you haven't written yet.
14. The take you didn't record is already recorded — scroll back
    through everything you played.
15. Step back into any moment of the session WITH the settings
    it had then.
16. Export any pass as a MIDI file and drop it in your DAW.
17. Loop the last four bars you just played and jam on top of
    yourself.
18. Load a MIDI file and let the whole grid treat it like a
    player.

### Discover without deciding
19. Deal 64 ready-made machines, all playing YOUR chord — keep
    the ones that surprise you.
20. Tap RANDOMIZE until something makes you grin; press MUTATE
    for variations of it.
21. Light up three machines at once and hear them as a band
    before committing.
22. Browse presets that perform your material live while you
    shop.

### Live performers
23. Stutter, freeze, and kill — a performance row built for
    fingers, danger increasing left to right.
24. Cue the next section and watch it sweep in on the bar.
25. Re-point a whole part at a different keyboard mid-song, one
    tap.
26. Two players, two controllers, zero channel gymnastics.

### The clever-simple stuff
27. An arp that breathes — rests and ties instead of relentless
    sixteenths.
28. Type a scale, exclude the chord — fills that can NEVER hit a
    wrong note.
29. One synth, two sounds: send one line out on a different
    channel.
30. Change the chord, and EVERYTHING — riffs, drums, captured
    phrases — changes with it. It's always your harmony.

### More: texture and motion
31. Echoes that run through what's after them — each repeat
    shorter, thinner, or re-tuned.
32. One machine, two layers: the straight line on synth A, the
    dressed-up version on synth B.
33. A pattern that climbs an octave over eight bars — draw it
    once, it breathes forever.
34. Filter sweeps that follow how busy your playing is — calm
    hands, calm sound.
35. Build a chord one note at a time, each arrival blooming into
    harmony.
36. Rake chords like a guitarist — up, down, or alternating,
    with a human tilt.
37. Low notes pulse slow, high notes sparkle fast — one chord,
    a whole texture.
38. Draw your groove — push the snare late, the hats early, by
    hand, per step.
39. Paint which synth each step comes out of — hocket as
    geometry.
40. Mute parts on a drawn schedule — arrangement that breathes
    without touching a machine.

### More: writing and rigs
41. Sketch a progression, capture a melody against it, then swap
    the chords — the melody re-voices itself.
42. Works with generative apps: feed their chords in, make their
    output follow YOUR harmony.
43. Two-handed duet on one keyboard — left hand chords, right
    hand melody, two inputs, zero setup.
44. Nineteen notes per octave? Your synth's tuning decides the
    pitches — we just send the notes.
45. Sixteen scenes on one strip — verse to chorus is one tap,
    and it lands on the bar.
46. Everything you do is undoable, loops fall silent instead of
    exploding, and one button takes the scene back.
47. Watch a machine think — input at the top, the pattern engine
    in the middle, what comes out below.
48. Every knob explains itself, in plain words, right where you
    touch it.
49. Same seed, same magic — reload the session and it plays back
    identically, note for note.
50. Sculpt one machine while it plays, then deal its best
    moments across eight rows — a ladder of variations built by
    ear.

### More: the little joys
51. Double-tap the reel and the last thing that made you smile
    is saved.
52. The pass browser tells you where things changed — "ARP RATE"
    in purple, right on the moment.
53. Freeze the whole piece into a hanging chord; release it on
    the downbeat.
54. Send everything to every synth at once — the wall, for one
    glorious bar.
55. Ten seconds: one cell, one arp, done. Ten minutes: a machine
    nobody else has. Same grid.
